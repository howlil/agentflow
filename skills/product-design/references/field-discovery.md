# Field discovery and adoption

Use for customer delivery, unfamiliar business workflows, first deployments or adoption problems. Scale discovery to decisions that can change scope; do not force an enterprise questionnaire on a small feature.

Trace current workflow → friction → proposed change → accepted outcome → sustained use. Separate observed facts, stakeholder statements and assumptions. Identify the actual user, decision maker, acceptance owner, data owner and operator only where their authority affects delivery.

Establish the current workaround and baseline when available; define what improvement would justify the change. Ask for missing facts only when they change scope or correctness. Do not invent ROI, volume, legal obligations or numeric targets. Validate sector-specific constraints with authoritative sources and domain owners.

Check feasibility through available integration/data access, representative data quality, environment restrictions, budget and deadline. Distinguish customer-controlled prerequisites from engineering tasks. Make unresolved access and contradictory stakeholder requirements visible before promising delivery.

Choose the smallest pilot that completes a useful workflow. Define acceptance examples, who can accept them, and what evidence is needed. Include human fallback for uncertain or high-impact automation. A demo or green CI alone does not establish customer acceptance.

Plan training, workflow transition, support ownership and handover only when required for use. After rollout, compare observed usage/outcomes with the agreed criteria; capture feedback that changes the product contract. Do not silently expand the pilot into a new backlog.

Persist these decisions in the existing product/task contract. Keep one authoritative requirement record; link to technical and operational owners instead of duplicating their documents.
